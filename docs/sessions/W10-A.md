# W10-A handoff: Share cards

## Status

complete — cards render, the fan-out enqueues them at the reveal, the share page and both image
routes serve them behind reveal gating, and the required tests pass. Three spec issues for Z, below.

## Summary

Every settled player of a daily round gets a share card. `src/cards/` renders the result, teaser and
round cards at all three variant sizes with satori and resvg-wasm in the Worker, from the Paper card
artboards' own numbers, the five static TTFs the design names, the mascot poses inlined as SVG, and
the long-text stepping rule. `build.ts` turns D1 into the model (reveal-gated, amounts only if the
player asked for them, the stray streak at 2 or more, no handle once an account is deleted), the
`cards` queue consumer renders and stores at the pinned R2 keys and gives each player's card its
opaque `share_cards` row, and the fan-out runs where the outcome becomes durable: `onModeFinal` for
Free and for a Stakes mode that finalised without a proposal, and the indexer's Stakes proposal
handler at the reveal itself.

`GET /s/:shareId` serves the share page with OG and Farcaster mini app embed tags and a Play link
carrying the sharer's ref code and the share ID; `GET /cards/:shareId/:variant.png` and
`GET /rounds/:id/card/:variant.png` serve the images from R2, render on demand when a key is
missing, and 404 until the mode reveals. A deletion re-renders the same keys without the handle, and
this colo's cached copies are dropped on every re-render.

## Deviations

- **satori 0.32.0, not the current 0.62.0.** From 0.33 satori imports `harfbuzzjs`, whose emscripten
  glue calls `WebAssembly.instantiate` on bytes at import time; workerd refuses that ("Wasm code
  generation disallowed by embedder"), so a newer satori cannot load in a Worker at all, with
  `satori/standalone` either. 0.32.0 is the last release without it and takes its layout engine as a
  module (`init(yoga.wasm)`), which workerd does allow.
- **The fonts are imported as base64, not as files.** `wrangler.jsonc` (W10-D's) would need a `Data`
  module rule for a `.ttf` import, and Vite — which builds the Workers tests — resolves one to a URL
  string instead. `assets/fonts/build.mjs` writes `src/cards/fonts.gen.ts` (353 KB of base64, about
  265 KB of TTF); both bundlers treat that identically. The TTFs stay in the repo as the source.
  Inter is instanced at `opsz` 24, not its default 14, because Paper renders with `opsz: auto` and at
  14 the refund card's why line came out wide enough to wrap a line the export keeps on one.
- **No migration: `0023` is unused and still reserved.** Whether a card image exists is a question
  R2 answers (`BUCKET.get`), so the `rendered_at` column the first half of this session planned
  earns nothing. `share_cards` as it stands holds everything the routes need, and `r2_prefix` is now
  written. W10-D's `0024` is unaffected.
- **The pixel comparison runs against committed fixtures**, each export box-filtered to a fifth of
  the card by `test/cards/fixtures/build.mjs`: the exports are ~300 KB each and a Workers test has no
  filesystem, so every reference byte has to be bundled into the test. The fixture set is 10 of the
  54 card exports — enough for every distinct layout, not every state.
- **Mean pixel error is a bound, not a fingerprint.** At a fifth scale a card is mostly background
  and the residual error (the two text engines' advance widths drifting along a line) is as large as
  the error between two different states' cards: a win rendered against the headcount-tie fixture
  scores 13.6 where the tie's own card scores 12.6. So `pixel.test.ts` bounds the difference
  (mae < 16, over < 14%) and `layout.test.ts` tells the states apart — ink-band positions within
  6 px and the accent's share within half a point, with five negative controls.
- **The `embed` and `square` refund title sizes are inferred.** Paper gave the `og` refund artboards
  a 72 px title (confirmed); `embed` 76 and `square` 92 keep the same ratio to their result lines and
  no fixture covers them. The `embed` geometry as a whole was derived from the exports (its bands
  match within 1 px) rather than read off the artboard, as is `flockWidth`, the five-sheep loss art
  (430/490/300 px: measured on the `og` export, proportional for the other two).
- **The teaser's big line breaks between its two sentences**, as the artboard draws it, rather than
  wrapping on width.
- **Stepping is decided from estimated advance widths**, not a trial render: a daily round renders
  three cards per settled player inside five minutes, so a render per step is too expensive. The
  tables are calibrated to four widths measured off the exports (`fit.test.ts`) and run 2–4% wide,
  because satori's text is a shade wider than the browser's that drew the artboards and an
  under-estimate clips a card. Two consequences: the Stakes amount card lands at 72 px where the
  artboard sets 76 (at 76 the line does not in fact fit beside the sheep here) and the round card's
  line at 80 px where the artboard sets 88; both are inside the layout test's tolerance. Stepping is
  four pixels at a time and never past the floor, so `og`'s 42 px question bottoms out at 34 rather
  than exactly 32 (the result line reaches 56 exactly from all three sizes).
- **The veto re-render arrives through the outcome that replaces the vetoed one.** The veto handler
  returns statements the dispatcher commits after it, so there is no "after the D1 commit" point
  inside it: a vetoed settlement is refunded by the `RoundRefunded` (reason 5) in the same
  transaction, which is final and runs the fan-out again at the same keys, and a vetoed refund
  proposal re-renders at its re-proposal (the guardian releases the hold first; until then the
  provisional refund card stands, which is what it was).
- **The share page is a small self-contained page**, on the tokens and the artboards' copy and
  structure (headline, card, "Play today's Flocked", "See the round"/"See the reveal"), not matched
  pixel-for-pixel to `cards/share-page/*`: no screenshot test covers it, and W11-D owns the unfurl
  e2e (`e2e/ui/share.spec.ts`). Its Play link is `/?ref=<ref_code>&share=<shareId>`; W11-D keeps both
  in the client for the 7-day window.
- **A void entry in a settled mode renders the refunded title with no why line.** No refund rule and
  no artboard covers an individual void (the mode settled; only that entry was dropped), and its
  stake did come back.
- **The edge purge is this colo's.** `caches.default.delete` is all a Worker has; other colos keep a
  copy until `s-maxage` (a day) runs out. A zone-wide purge needs the zone purge API and a token the
  Worker does not have — an owner action if the day is too long.
- **`CARD_REFUND_LINES`, `refundKeepsSplit` and a USDC formatter are duplicated** from
  `apps/web/src/features/reveal/copy.ts` and `apps/web/src/ui/amounts.ts`, which a Worker cannot
  import. They belong in `@flocked/shared`, which neither W10-A nor W10-C owns this wave.
- **Over size M**, and over the handoff's ~120 lines. Matching the frozen artboards took the whole
  budget, so the session stopped `partial` at a green commit and emitted `docs/prompts/W10-A.2.md`
  (plan.md 2.6); the owner chose to run that continuation here, so this handoff covers both halves
  and the prompt file stays as the record.

## Spec issues

- **Who creates a teaser `share_cards` row.** A teaser has to exist before the reveal (the Sealed
  screen's share sheet links `/s/:shareId`), but no enqueue point in this session's scope writes
  one: the entry path is `routes/entries.ts`. `enqueueTeaserCard(env, roundId, mode, userId)` is
  built and tested; it needs one call when an entry is sealed. Spec heading: "Share cards and
  distribution" › Share surfaces.
- **Room-round share pages.** Cards render for room rounds (the `too_few_entrants` refund artboard
  uses a 2-player room round), but `/s/:shareId` and `/cards/:shareId/*.png` are public by design, so
  a room round's share page would publish member-only per-option data ("API" › Reveal gating). This
  session's answer: the fan-out is daily-rounds-only, so a room round never gets a `share_cards` row,
  and its round card renders on demand at `/rounds/:id/card/*.png` behind `requireRoundAccess`. Worth
  a line in the spec either way.
- **`/rounds/:id/card/:variant.png` names no mode**, but a card is per mode (and the R2 key has one).
  It serves the revealed mode, Free first, which is the client's default; a round whose Stakes mode
  revealed first still shows Free once Free reveals. The spec should either name the mode in the path
  or say which mode the round card is.

## Open issues

- The remaining 44 card exports have no fixture. `FIXTURES` in `test/cards/fixtures/build.mjs` and
  the `CASES` tables in `layout.test.ts` and `pixel.test.ts` extend to them; the states are listed in
  `docs/design/screens.md` "Share cards" and "Refund cards".
- Rendering costs about 150–350 ms a card. The `cards` consumer's batch is 10 messages × 3 variants
  = 30 renders, about 10 s of the Worker's 60 s `cpu_ms`, and Cloudflare autoscales consumers, so a
  1,200-player round should finish inside the five-minute target; nobody has measured it on real
  infrastructure yet (a stack or staging run would).
- `share_conversion` (the other half of the referral analytics) is W11-D's: the client sends the
  share ID back on the account's first entry.
